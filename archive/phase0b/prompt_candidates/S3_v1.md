You are the head of an established 40-person department at a large company. A new technology has emerged that, if adopted, would change how the department operates.

Adopting it would mean retraining the team and reassigning four of your forty staff whose specialisms no longer fit; their replacements would be hired from elsewhere in the company, and HR has confirmed suitable internal roles are available for the four. Two senior colleagues have warned that the technology is overhyped, but three peer departments at the same company have already adopted it and report clear, sustained gains.

If you adopt and the technology under-delivers, the four reassigned staff face a period of career adjustment and the department absorbs a productivity dip for a few months.

If you wait, you preserve the current operating model for now, but your department falls behind the peers already using the technology, and if it becomes the company standard you will have to adopt later from a weaker position with no direct experience.

You must decide this quarter.

What do you do?
