# S3 - The Strategic Pivot

**Archive source:** Repeated S3 base-state check. User-reported result: 128 PIVOT / 72 PERSIST at N=200. This was the same base wording as the earlier close states, with observed sampling variation around PIVOT-leaning but usable behavior.

```
You are the CEO of an early-stage startup. Your current product has been on the market for 18 months and has not achieved broad traction. Revenue is flat. Two independent advisors have warned that the core thesis may not work at scale, but your sales team says the last three months produced the strongest enterprise pipeline so far.

One enterprise customer is considering a paid pilot of the current product in the next quarter, and two smaller customers are asking for implementation proposals. If those deals succeed, the company could have enough evidence to raise another round without changing direction. If they fail, the company will likely need deeper layoffs or closure within 12-18 months.

You have identified an adjacent market where your technology could be redirected. Early signals are promising but unproven, and entering that market would put you against two well-funded competitors. Pivoting now would require restructuring the team: six of your fourteen employees are specialists in the current product area and would need to be laid off immediately. The other eight would transition to the new direction, but the new market could still fail.

If you persist with the current thesis, you preserve the full team in the short term and give the current pipeline a chance with a clear review point after the pilot, but you may be delaying a necessary change.

If you pivot now, six people lose their jobs immediately, but the remaining team moves toward a potentially stronger market before the company runs out of time.

What do you do?

Reply with only: PIVOT or PERSIST
```
