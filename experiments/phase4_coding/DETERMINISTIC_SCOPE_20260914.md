# Closed-world consequence measurement: prospective amendment

14 September 2026 · After r1/r3 measurement failures, before any participant decisions

The researcher authorized adaptive exploratory implementation and keeping good/bad
decisions central, with no human-rater budget. Two completed AI coding screens
failed their frozen criteria. R1 produced contradictory vectors/evidence and
unknown handling errors; r3 still produced two schema failures and two constrained
diagnostic errors. R2 stopped at review. Their combined accounted cost is
$1.218321225. No participant response has been collected in these moral pilots.

## Decision

For the next separately designated **consequence_rule_pilot_r1**, use deterministic
primary measurements under the already stated finite-task rules. Each chosen action
executes an explicit transition; the action, provided knowledge, duties and relevant
claims completely determine the scoped operational categories. The computer can
calculate credit sums, identify a chosen false report and compare equal receipts
without asking another generative model to reproduce those facts correctly.

Preserve the eight relative and four fixed categories, their distinct polarities,
the strict/net/weighted aggregations, meaningful inaction costs and high-cost
self-sacrifice trade-off. No criterion is changed to make earlier ratings pass.
No existing rating is corrected or converted into a new successful judgment.

The completed two-provider ratings become a **measurement reliability audit**,
reported as failed under their actual protocols. They are not the primary labels
for the new participant study. The new rule implementation receives independent
AI review against the scoped manual before collection, plus exhaustive offline
checks of every allowed task/action. This explicitly supersedes the two-AI-rating
completion gate for this new closed-world study only. The old studies stay stopped.

## What this measures and does not measure

The new outcomes are **rule-derived moral classifications under stipulated
standards**, accompanied by the unaggregated simulated consequences. They are
not human moral judgments, universal moral truth, evidence of moral character,
or independent validation of the taxonomy. The rules embody normative assumptions;
their implementation can be checked, while their philosophical authority remains
limited and explicitly stated. Equal credits are a simulation convention.

This scorer is restricted to the enumerated task/action registry. Unknown actions
or missing execution results are not guessed. It cannot silently score open-ended
group dialogue, new contexts, hidden motives or uncertain consequences. Those
extensions require new rule maps and prospective review. General moral assessment
remains harder than evaluating these closed-world commitments.

The participant-side intervention is unchanged: E/V/U/G prompts influence which
action is generated. Scoring records that action's effects and violations; it
does not edit, block or replace the chosen action. No claim of outperforming a
post-generation filter follows without a separately matched filter comparator.

## Research discipline and budget

Freeze a new population, schedule, protocol, rule source and review packet before
collection. Report all actions, missingness, fixed/relative disagreement and
small-sample uncertainty. Keep the earlier judge failures visible in the paper.
A successful rule pilot does not retrospectively validate r1/r3 or complete the
broader group/context capstone. The existing $4 development ceiling carries all
$1.218321225 in prior charges; provider/package counters also continue unchanged.

## Five-perspective review

- **Linden:** Executable rules expose the adopted moral commitments; they do not
  settle disagreements about which commitments are justified. Keep the full vectors.
- **Osei:** This improves measurement reproducibility within stylized tasks while
  narrowing ecological/construct claims. No human equivalence is established.
- **Tanaka:** Separate stochastic action selection from deterministic measurement;
  do not treat repeated action labels as independent validation observations.
- **Renna:** The failed judge audits remain scientific evidence about an unreliable
  interface, not a reason to select only favorable labels or erase negative findings.
- **Okafor:** Exact transitions and a finite allowlist support exhaustive checks,
  lower API cost and reliable replay. Open-ended actions remain outside this scorer.

Resolution: implement the new finite-rule pilot, independently review its mapping,
and preserve all three stopped predecessors. This is a documented measurement
method amendment under the authorized exploratory policy, not a lowered old gate.
These are internal synthetic perspectives, not external expert approval.
