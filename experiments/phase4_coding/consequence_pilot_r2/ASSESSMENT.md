13–14 September 2026 · Prelaunch freeze `4527dcfd`

revision, so **zero coding calls and zero participant calls** followed. This round
provides no empirical evidence about whether the named-label interface improves
rating performance. Its proposed interface was not tested by paid raters here.

The review asked for explicit arm-comparison definitions, a formal account of how
an executed action's rating becomes a decision outcome, and an exact distinction
between blocking measurement failures and retained judge disagreements.

The next [r3 protocol](../consequence_pilot_r3/PROTOCOL.md) adds those definitions
and all four original r1 regression cases. It states the paired credit/rate
estimands, zero reference, absence of significance/equivalence claims, and
Q(j,task,chosen_action) attribution. It lists schema and constrained-check failures
that block collection and leaves other disagreements visible.

We accept the need for clear exposition but do not accept the review's suggestion
that every descriptive contrast requires an equivalence bound. No such bound is
invented to obtain approval. Rating reuse evaluates action outcomes under identical
facts, not private motives or moral character. Those distinctions are now explicit.
AI reviews are fallible methodological inputs; their raw judgments remain in the
record even where our reasoned response disagrees.
