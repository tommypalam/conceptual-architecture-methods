# Phase 4 amendment: human raters deferred

13 September 2026 · Prospective scope amendment, before moral collection

The researcher stated that there is no budget for human raters and instructed us
to drop that requirement for now and proceed with implementation. This explicitly
supersedes the human manual-review and human-coded validation prerequisites in the
current completion plan. No participant recruitment or assumption of unpaid work
is made. Original specification v0.1, thesis v0.6 and frozen studies remain intact.

The good/bad decision experiment remains central. Its revised deliverable is
**AI-assisted moral evaluation under explicit, versioned standards**, alongside
separately measured simulated consequences. It can complete this amended scope
without human raters. It cannot support claims of human moral consensus, a
human-validated taxonomy, intrinsic ethical understanding or human resemblance.

## Replacement measurement safeguards

1. Draft and independently AI-review the manual, including authored calibration
   examples and exclusion rules. Freeze it before new capstone collection.
2. Use two provider judges with separate calls, blind to arm/profile/model and
   each other's judgments. Supply the institutional facts needed for relative
   scoring and the same fixed standard in every condition.
3. Require category-specific evidence from facts, selected actions and recorded
   state changes. Keep intentions, expected consequences and realised effects
   distinct. Run deterministic checks for quantities and transition consistency.
4. Preserve each judgment, disagreement and unscorable item. Report each judge's
   estimates and conservative disagreement bounds; no forced consensus gold set.
5. Use separate development and held-out check items. Failure produces a documented
   revision and new checks, not retroactive reliability claims. Exact sampling,
   metrics, limits and stopping rules belong in the future collection protocol.

Different providers may share errors and normative biases. AI agreement measures
consistency under this rubric; it does not independently establish moral truth.
Human behavioural comparison remains a separate unresolved research question.

## Five-perspective review

Decision: replace the unaffordable human-rating gate with explicitly limited
AI-assisted measurement, preserving the central moral experiment.

- **Linden:** Keep both moral standards explicit. Consensus is not justification;
  contested and mixed cases must remain visible.
- **Osei:** This loses human grounding. Report that limit and retain separate
  behavioural-comparison status rather than substituting AI opinions for humans.
- **Tanaka:** Report disagreement, missingness and cluster uncertainty. Two votes
  are not two independent samples of a human population.
- **Renna:** Factual consequences provide a useful independent check on judge
  assertions, but cannot validate all normative category boundaries.
- **Okafor:** The amended study is executable within the existing API envelope;
  use finite transitions, meaningful inaction costs and auditable decisions.

Resolution: proceed under the researcher's amendment, with narrower measurement
claims. The remaining disagreement concerns external validity, which is disclosed
and deferred rather than turned into an unaffordable completion requirement.
These are internal synthetic review perspectives, not external expert approval.
