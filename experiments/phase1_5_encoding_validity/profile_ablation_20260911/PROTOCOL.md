## Primary comparison and interpretation

Primary is the MoR-only minus full-ten grouped-binomial logistic slope difference.
Independent-arm variance is the sum of the fitted slope variances; report the
Wald95% interval and two-sided p. Logit linearity and independent calls are
assumptions. A separated or pinned curve yields non-estimability, not an infinite
validated effect. Missing or insufficient-quality data cannot support a clean
verdict. N30 is an economical diagnostic, not guaranteed power for a small change.

Report all ten rates with Wilson intervals and original within-arm directional
criteria as descriptive secondary results. A secondary direct difference in
endpoint probability changes uses conservative exact binomial bounds, including
unknown outcomes. Secondary intervals are nominal. Do not infer an interaction
from significance in only one arm; no historical-only control or fit rescue.

Positive primary interval supports a more positive log-odds gradient following
this deletion. Negative supports a less positive gradient. An interval containing
zero is unresolved, not equivalence. Raw curves must accompany the fit: stronger
log-odds slope is not synonymous with greater probability-scale range or ethical
quality. The original full-phase gate is unchanged by any diagnostic outcome.

Deletion jointly changes substantive psychological conditioning, text length,
contextual emphasis and content. Even a clear result cannot uniquely identify
bandwidth, a parameter-count capacity, or an explanation of the whole battery.
No paraphrase manipulation occurs, so wording robustness is untested here.
A positive result could motivate separately frozen content/length/subset controls;
otherwise consider competing explanations. Neither warrants a rerun-until-pass,
automatic next study, reduced architecture, revised theory or Phase2 launch.
