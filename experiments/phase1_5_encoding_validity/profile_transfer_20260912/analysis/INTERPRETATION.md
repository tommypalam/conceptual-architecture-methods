## What changed in the evidence

MoR's known direction extends beyond the repeated mean-valued profile to new
complete backgrounds. Its aggregate rates22.5/40/80% are ordered at the three
sampled levels; both collection blocks have positive endpoint effects. This is
stronger evidence of transfer for the tested parameter/dilemma than another
repetition of one familiar cell. It is not universal responsiveness across
individual backgrounds or unseen dilemmas.

MS also has a positive average endpoint effect across these new backgrounds.
However, its rates **7.5/2.5/35% are not monotonic**: the midpoint is below the low
endpoint. This is prominent evidence limiting a claim of graded MS encoding,
not a detail to conceal under the local endpoint pass. The observed dip itself
has not been assigned a separate significance test. This run does not resolve
the earlier fixed-profile repeatability discrepancies or identify their cause.

RE does not meet the local criterion. Its adjusted interval includes substantial
positive and negative effects, and its block effects change sign. This is not
proof of zero RE influence; it does not support a reliable predicted average
transfer effect at this precision. Its inclusion therefore did not establish
the hoped-for pair of opposite MoR/RE responses in S3. Do not remove RE, change
its meaning or optimise away contrary backgrounds on this basis.
