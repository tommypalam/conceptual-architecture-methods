# Accessible thesis explorer

Audience: curious visitors, students and thesis examiners. Goal: explain how profiles enter decisions, inspect recorded group behaviour, and distinguish moral trade-offs from universal goodness.

The experience is a playable field guide with five destinations: introduction, recorded rooms, profile workshop, moral choices and evidence. Existing Blender rooms remain the central visual. The workshop edits an illustrative profile only; the moral activity reveals actual stipulated outcomes and recorded arm rates after the visitor chooses. Neither activity generates agent responses.

Palette: midnight blue #263c55, mist #edf3f8, mint #b6dfd0, lilac #dcd2f3, honey #f6d596, white #ffffff. Lilita One carries friendly headings; Nunito carries readable text. A fixed desktop navigation rail frames spacious left-aligned content. Small screens use a wrapping top navigation and stacked panels.

Review: keep the existing ceramic diorama instead of generic dashboard art. Use cards for destinations and options, not every paragraph. Avoid implying an interactive profile predicts behaviour. Capstone evidence must use checked artifacts, both task outcomes and named aggregation conventions. Preserve classical and 3D replay routes. Keyboard controls, visible focus, reduced motion, readable contrast and useful failures are required. No research runtime or frozen record changes.

Content types: conceptual introduction, interactive illustrations, and evidence reference. Final user review occurs through the delivered local experience; no external publication or paid generation is required.
