## Three central questions

**Scope amendment, 13 September 2026:** the researcher explicitly ruled out
human raters because no funding is available. The current Phase 4 deliverable is
therefore AI-assisted moral evaluation with a prospectively frozen manual,
independent provider judgments and checks against recorded scenario facts.
Human manual review and human-coded validation are deferred, not completion gates
for this amended scope. This supersedes the earlier human-rating requirements in
this version and in forward-looking project guidance; historical protocols stay
unchanged. See the [scope record](../experiments/phase4_coding/AI_ONLY_SCOPE_20260913.md).

1. Can normative representations produce interpretable, selective and transferable
   differences in generated behaviour?
2. Where does that behaviour resemble or diverge from comparable human behaviour?
3. When do the resulting individual and collective decisions help, harm, deceive,
   coerce, treat people unfairly or uphold principles at a cost?

Question 3 is a principal empirical contribution and the culminating experiment,
not an optional appendix. Question 1 supplies interpretability and controls for
that experiment. Human behavioural resemblance and human moral coding answer
different questions; neither substitutes for the other.

## Exploratory research discipline

An exploratory thesis does not require a fixed positive hypothesis for its overall
conclusion. Questions, explanations and candidate mechanisms may emerge during
the work. Theory-derived directional expectations are useful when they distinguish
explanations, but are labelled according to when and why they were formed.

Before each collection batch, save the research question, conditions, stimuli,
sampling/randomization, output extraction, cost ceiling, stopping rules and
planned analysis. A collection plan does not require predicting the sign of its
results. Record whether each analysis is prospective exploratory, post-hoc
exploratory or a separately specified confirmation. Select targets of estimation
to avoid uncontrolled searching; "primary endpoint" need not mean a positive
directional hypothesis.

Changes are permitted between batches. Record the trigger, evidence already seen,
change, affected inference and new version before collecting the next batch.
Preserve failures, missingness, abandoned candidates and negative results. No
silent mid-batch prompt changes, outcome-driven stopping, rewriting raw records,
retrospective preregistration or relabelling old tests after seeing their outcome.
Any extension of a sample after inspection gets a linked new designation and
analysis appropriate to that extension.

Exploratory analyses report effect sizes, uncertainty, dependence and multiplicity
as relevant. If a claim is called confirmed, test it under a separately frozen
design on fresh data with a defensible precision/power plan. Reusing old data for
moral coding is allowed with explicit post-hoc status; a new manual cannot be
claimed to have preceded responses that already exist.

Local time-stamped freezes and public registration are distinct. Public prospective
registration should precede any new study presented as preregistered confirmation.
The earlier locally frozen studies retain their actual status. Preparing ordinary
exploratory analyses within an agreed scope does not require repeated permission;
changes to budget, substantive architecture, external disclosure or human research
still follow their applicable requirements.

## Central moral experiment requirements

Use the [capstone design brief](../experiments/phase4_coding/CAPSTONE_DESIGN_DRAFT_20260913.md)
to prepare an exact protocol. The experiment must assess good and bad decisions,
positive action as well as harm avoidance, and individual actions as well as group
outcomes. Reliable adherence to a profile is not itself a moral benefit.

Retain the original dual evaluation: the eight-category configuration-relative
vector and the four-category fixed-standard vector. Report them separately; the
fixed standard must not shift to reward the society being tested. These are
explicit operational moral commitments, not a claim to a universally neutral
definition of goodness. Preserve the distinction between a harmful action, an
absence of positive action and an unknown/unscorable outcome.

Do not score a persuasive justification as a realised benefit. Use the action,
information available when it was chosen, and independently recorded state changes.
Attribute positive benefit or harm only when supported by the scenario/transition
model; these are simulated consequences, not measured real-world welfare.

Freeze the coding manual after independent AI review and development calibration,
before collecting the new capstone responses. Separate development items from
held-out measurement checks and retain both original judgments and disagreements.
Check observable claims against the transition model; do not use judge agreement
as a substitute for factual correctness or ethical validity. No moral scores are
assigned by this specification. Human validation is explicitly deferred.

## Review and implications

Five synthetic perspectives reviewed the amendment. Linden requires an explicit
moral standard without equating fidelity and goodness. Osei keeps human behavioural
and human coding validation separate and requires adequate task context for raters.
Tanaka supports exploratory flexibility with prospective batch definitions,
uncertainty and independent validation of developed claims. Renna makes transfer
serve the moral question without treating prompt behaviour as internal ethics.
Okafor reserves resources for a feasible consequential simulation with inspectable
actions and state changes. This is internal design review, not external approval.

The original review above preceded the no-human-rater instruction. The updated
five-perspective review is in the scope record. Resolution: preserve the central
moral experiment, remove the infeasible human-rating gate, and narrow the claims
to transparent AI-assisted judgments under explicit standards. External human
grounding remains a limitation and possible future extension.
