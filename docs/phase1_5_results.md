# Functional normative parameterization: results and interpretation

Manuscript draft, 12 September 2026. This account reports completed studies; it does not claim public preregistration. Frozen local protocols distinguish prospective confirmations from exploratory analyses. The researcher accepted closure around the limited possibility claim after observing the evidence.

## Intervention and scope

PARIA represents an agent using ten normatively motivated coordinates, sampled from Beta marginals linked by a Gaussian copula. The encoding-validity studies tested whether intervening on those coordinates changed decisions in fixed dilemmas. In the later studies, other coordinates were held constant within each paired background while the focal coordinate varied. All ten coordinates remained in the analysis.

Profiles were supplied before the behavioural model generated its response. The application retained raw responses and parsed decision labels; it did not apply an ethical rule to replace generated decisions. Consequently, measured profile effects concern decision generation in this application. They do not isolate the foundation model's latent computations or its training-time alignment mechanisms. The tested model was `gpt-5.4-mini-2026-03-17`; the later studies used temperature 1 and a 600-token output cap.

## Independent confirmation of Procedural Dependence

An exploratory all-ten study identified Procedural Dependence (PD) as a promising coordinate. A separate confirmation froze two endpoint predictions before collecting 100 responses across 25 new, unselected backgrounds in neutral context. Increasing PD from .1 to .9 increased FORMAL_REPORT in S2 from 0% to 80%, a paired difference of 80 percentage points (simultaneous 95% CI [35.4, 95.2]; Holm-adjusted exact p=0.00000381). In S3, WAIT increased from 16% to 84%, a difference of 68 points (simultaneous 95% CI [22.9, 88.2]; adjusted p=0.0000153). All responses were valid; no background was excluded. Draw and schedule seeds were 20261012 and 20261013. [Frozen confirmation and verification](../experiments/phase1_5_encoding_validity/structural_encoding_20260912/pd_confirmation/analysis/REPORT.md).

These effects support reproducible, context-dependent influence by a normatively defined input. Interpretation remains conditional: the dilemmas were previously studied, and new profiles do not establish transfer to new tasks. S3 waiting may reflect caution or preservation as well as procedural concern. Neither action is treated as inherently morally better.

## Graded effects and delivery dependence

The later all-ten follow-up allocated 17,250 requests to gradients and wording comparisons in neutral context. Fifty new backgrounds were sampled; the first 25 were used for five-level gradients across ten parameters, three dilemmas and three representations. All 50 were used for wording comparisons. Draw, schedule and analysis seeds were 20261020, 20261021 and 20261022. Of 17,247 saved responses, 17,234 were valid. Three further dispatches lacked responses. Missing and invalid slots prevented the frozen complete-data bootstrap; the prespecified exact paired sensitivity retained all 180 contrasts in Holm correction, assigning p=1 to eight incomplete contrasts. [Full assessment and frozen result](../experiments/phase1_5_encoding_validity/all_ten_assessment_20260912/ASSESSMENT.md).

Across PD values .1, .3, .5, .7 and .9, canonical S2 reporting rates were 4%, 4%, 16%, 40% and 68%; S3 adoption rates were 100%, 80%, 64%, 40% and 32%. Both endpoint contrasts survived Holm180 correction. These ordered observed curves complement the independent endpoint confirmation; they are not proof of a globally monotonic response function. Verbal PD endpoint and interior contrasts also survived correction in both dilemmas, providing local evidence across two delivery forms. The originally unspecified PD/simple-task signs retain their exploratory origin; the independent confirmation remains a separate prospective test.

Canonical MoR/S3 also had an ordered observed curve and a corrected endpoint effect, but MoR primarily concerns response style. Numeric-only RT/S3 significantly opposed its original directional prediction. MS/S2 was not ordered and no MS contrast survived Holm180. These findings prevent interpreting any significant response to a parameter as successful normative encoding.

Broad robustness remained unestablished. The original battery yielded 8/30 equivalent paraphrase cells against 24 required. The new fresh-profile comparison yielded 1/30 under the frozen conservative paired calculation, with five incomplete cells and 3/30 under an independent-TOST sensitivity. These methods and estimands differ, so the counts are not a direct trend. Limited equivalence precision and genuine wording effects must be distinguished. Both eligible legacy MoR representation-retention comparisons failed; PD's original-rule ineligibility reflected absent original predictions. PD's useful verbal effects therefore do not validate all representations.

The separate 200-item blind explanation audit failed its aggregate thresholds. PD was the strongest active-coordinate signal (11/20 correct; supplementary Holm10 p=.03). This is corroborative evidence with limited precision and repeated-background dependence. Self-explanations are not direct observations of the model's decision mechanism.

## Conclusion

The evidence supports an initial, conditional demonstration of functional normative parameterization: explicit normative profiles can systematically shape generated AI decisions, with replicated PD effects providing the clearest case. The contribution is limited but positive. The complete ten-parameter architecture, its five-concept mapping and broad expression robustness are not validated. Ethical understanding, persistent internalization and improved moral performance remain unestablished.

The researcher's decision to close Phase 1.5 around this limited claim was made after the results. It neither alters the original battery outcome nor establishes a new confirmatory pass threshold. Collective dynamics, behavioural retrodiction and moral-performance scoring remain future empirical work.
