# Concepts as Architecture

## A Probabilistic Framework for Encoding Political-Ethical Concepts in LLM-Based Agents

**Tommaso Piero Palamenga — Bocconi University**

### Abstract

Can explicit representations of ethical dispositions systematically shape an
artificial agent's decisions during generation? This paper introduces PARIA, a
framework for investigating how political-ethical concepts influence individual
and collective behaviour in large-language-model agents. Drawing on psychological
accounts of freedom, justice, authority, care and loyalty, PARIA represents
individual dispositions through ten bounded parameters with Beta distributions
coupled by a Gaussian copula. Profiles condition decision generation, while the
application preserves the resulting choices without subsequent ethical answer
substitution. Matched experiments compare profiles and institutional contexts
across individual dilemmas and multi-agent deliberations.

An independent confirmation with 400 fresh profiles produced 4,800 individual
decisions and 300 group simulations. All six prespecified individual contrasts
between profile-conditioned and context-only agents survived correction for
multiple comparisons; group evidence was more limited, with one of seven
secondary contrasts surviving correction. Separate targeted experiments
replicated effects of procedural dependence, supporting a local correspondence
between a theoretically motivated disposition and generated behaviour. Broader
tests did not establish validity across all ten parameters. An exploratory
comparison of numerical profiles with information-matched prose established
neither a clear presentation effect nor equivalence. Recognition screening also
found that alternative versions of five classic social-psychological benchmarks
remained identifiable, limiting their use as controls for benchmark familiarity.

These findings support a conditional demonstration of functional normative
parameterization: explicit profiles can systematically influence generated
decisions in the tested settings. They do not establish intrinsic ethical
understanding, human behavioural equivalence or improved moral outcomes. PARIA
provides a reproducible experimental framework for separating behavioural
influence from stronger claims about ethical competence, and for investigating
the specificity, robustness and transfer of normative representations in
artificial agents.

**Keywords:** normative AI; ethical parameterization; large language models;
agent-based simulation; political psychology; behavioural validation.
